## What a patta is, and what it is not

A patta is a revenue record. It records who the revenue authority holds to be in
possession of a parcel and liable for it. It is strong evidence and it is not a
title deed.

confirm the position as it stands now rather than repeating an older name.

The practical consequence is simple. An entry in the record supports a claim of
ownership. It does not settle one. A buyer still traces the documents behind the
entry.

## Self-acquired land and ancestral land

The distinction decides who has to sign.

- **Self-acquired land** is bought by the owner. Broadly, the owner decides what

## Why this matters to a buyer

A sale by one family member, of land in which several hold a share, can be
challenged afterwards. The buyer is the one holding the problem: the money has
moved, the possession may have changed, and the claim arrives years later.

The defences are procedural and they are boring, which is why they get skipped.

- Establish the family tree behind the current holder. {{VERIFY}} what document
  establishes succession and who issues it.
- Identify every person holding a share and obtain each one's consent in the form
  the law requires. {{VERIFY}} what form that is, including for a minor's share.
- Where a partition is claimed, obtain the partition document and check whether it
  required registration. {{VERIFY}} when a partition must be registered.
- Where any share holder is absent, unreachable or deceased, stop and take advice
  before proceeding.

## Questions to put to a seller, in writing

{{VERIFY}} Check this list with an advocate before publishing, and cut anything
that implies a legal conclusion.

- How did you come to hold this land: purchase, inheritance, partition, grant?
- Who else in the family holds or has held a share?
- Has the land ever been partitioned, and is that recorded?
- Is anyone in the chain deceased, and is the succession recorded?
- Has any family member ever raised a claim or sent a notice over this parcel?

A seller who answers these readily and in writing is telling you something. So is
one who does not.
