## Why this sequence, in this order

Each check narrows the risk left in the one before it. Confirming ownership
matters little if the land cannot legally be built on. Confirming classification
matters little if there is an unreleased loan against it. Do them in order and a
failure at any step ends the transaction cheaply.

{{VERIFY}} Confirm with a practising advocate that this ordering matches current
practice in Telangana, and note where Andhra Pradesh or Karnataka differs, since
land near the ORR can touch more than one revenue jurisdiction.

## Step one: who owns it

The question is not who is selling it. The question is who the record says owns
it, and whether the seller's chain of ownership to that record is unbroken.

- The current record of rights and the documents behind it. {{VERIFY}} the exact
  name of the record and the portal or office that issues it in Telangana today.
- The chain of prior transfers, going back far enough to cover any gap. {{VERIFY}}
  how far back the chain is customarily traced, and why.
- Where the land passed by inheritance rather than sale, the succession record.
  {{VERIFY}} what document establishes this and who issues it.

Ancestral land carries a specific risk: a family member with a share who never
consented to the sale can raise a claim afterwards. We cover this separately in
the article on patta and ancestral land history.

## Step two: what the land is classified as

Land near the ORR sits across several classifications, and the classification
decides what can be built, whether conversion is required, and whether the
transaction is permitted at all.

- The land use zone under the applicable master plan. {{VERIFY}} which authority's
  master plan governs the specific survey number, since the ORR corridor spans
  more than one planning jurisdiction.
- Whether the land is agricultural and requires conversion before non-agricultural
  use. {{VERIFY}} the current conversion process and the authority responsible.
- Whether any Government Order affects the parcel or the wider block. {{VERIFY}}
  where GOs are published and how to search for ones affecting a survey number.
- Categories that restrict or prohibit transfer: assigned land, endowment land,
  wakf land, government land, and land in a prohibited list. {{VERIFY}} the
  current list of restricted categories and where each register is published.

## Step three: what is charged against it

An encumbrance certificate shows registered transactions over a period. It is a
necessary check and not a sufficient one, because it shows what was registered,
not what was agreed privately.

- Pull the encumbrance certificate for a period long enough to cover the chain.
  {{VERIFY}} the period customarily pulled and the portal that issues it.
- Check for a subsisting mortgage and, where one was repaid, the release.
- Ask what is not on the certificate: unregistered agreements, family
  arrangements, oral partitions. {{VERIFY}} what weight each of these carries.

## Step four: whether it is in dispute

- Pending litigation over the parcel or the chain. {{VERIFY}} which court records
  are searchable online and how to search them by property.
- Any revenue proceeding or objection recorded against the entry.
- Physical possession: who is actually on the land, and whether that matches the
  record. Go and look. A boundary that does not match the survey sketch is a
  dispute that has not started yet.
